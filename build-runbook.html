01

Security — read this first

You said this is sensitive because it touches money. You are right, and the correct response is to give me less access, not more.

The one rule

Never paste a live secret into this chat. Not a password, not an API secret, not an OTP, not a bank detail. Not once, not "just to test".

This is structural, not a matter of trust. Everything typed here becomes part of a conversation transcript that persists, gets summarised, and may be reviewed. A secret that enters a transcript must be treated as compromised and rotated. The way to avoid that is never to put it here in the first place.

Every secret in this project is generated by you and entered by you, directly into the system that needs it. I will tell you exactly which field to paste it into. I never need to see it.

What I actually need from you

Two things. Both are low-risk, scoped, and revocable in one click.

The complete list of access I need
#AccessHow it is grantedWhy I need itRevoke
1 Shopify Collaborator access I send you a collaborator request code. You go to Settings → Users → Collaborators, approve it, and tick only the permission boxes listed in Section 02. Never your password. To build the theme, load products, configure the store and set up the order workflow. One click, any time. Store keeps running.
2 Google Analytics & Search Console Add my email as a user with Editor on Analytics and Full on Search Console. To install tracking correctly and submit the sitemap. Remove user, any time.

Optional, and only if we hit an integration problem: Razorpay test-mode keys (they begin rzp_test_). Test mode moves no real money and touches no real bank account. Even these are not needed for a normal Shopify install — the Razorpay app is configured by pasting keys into a field, and you can do that yourself while I watch the screen.

Never send me — and never send anyone who asks

  • Live Razorpay keys (rzp_live_…) or the webhook secret. You generate them, you paste them into Shopify. I never see them.
  • Any password — Shopify, Razorpay, Meta, Google, the domain registrar, email, anything.
  • Any OTP or 2FA code. There is no legitimate reason for me or anyone else to ask. If something asks you for an OTP that you did not just trigger yourself, stop and tell me.
  • Bank account credentials, net banking logins, UPI PIN, card numbers or CVV.
  • 2FA recovery codes or backup codes.
  • GST portal, income tax portal or FSSAI portal logins.
  • The client's KYC documents. Those go from the client to Razorpay, and nowhere else. Not through you, not through me.

Rules that apply for the whole build

  • Two-factor authentication on every account, switched on at creation, not before launch. Shopify, Razorpay, Meta, Google, the domain registrar and the store's email. You
  • Every account in the client's name, on the client's card. You are added as staff or collaborator, never the owner. You
  • Test mode until proven. No development work happens against live keys. Live keys go in once, at Stage 3, after the test matrix passes. Both
  • Nothing secret in the repository. Secret scanning on, .gitignore covering every env file, and no keys in commit messages, screenshots or issue text. Claude
  • Least privilege on staff accounts. Packing staff see orders, not settings, not payouts. Claude
  • Alerts on from day one — Razorpay email and SMS alerts for every settlement, refund and failed payment, sent to the client, not to you. You
  • My access reduced at handover to view-only, or removed entirely if the client prefers. You
  • Any secret that is ever exposed gets rotated immediately — not "watched", rotated. See the incident playbook in Section 11. Both

A note on what I am. I can write code, themes, configuration, copy and checklists, and I can tell you exactly what to click. I cannot complete a KYC, receive an OTP, verify a business with Meta, or log into a bank — and I should not be given the means to. Build the project so that remains true, and the worst credential mistake available to us is one that can be undone with a click.

Why collaborator and not staff. A Shopify collaborator account is designed for exactly this: it is free, it does not consume a staff seat, permissions are granular, it never involves sharing a password, and the client can revoke it in one click without disturbing anything. Ask for staff access only if the client's plan does not support collaborators.

Claude

Stop conditions

If any of rows 7, 10, 12 or 13 fails, stop the launch. Those four are the ones where a failure quietly costs the client money or creates a tax problem: a duplicate order, money that never lands, a broken invoice series, or stock sold twice. A failure there is not a snag to fix later.

Record each run in the repository with the date, who ran it, and the order and payment reference numbers. If a figure is ever questioned, that record is the answer.

05

Incident playbook

What to do when something goes wrong with money. Decided now, calmly, rather than at 10pm during a festival sale.

If this happens → do this, in this order
SituationFirst actionThen
Customer says they paid but has no order Find the payment in Razorpay by amount, time and phone number. Confirm it actually succeeded. If it succeeded: create the order manually, mark it paid against that payment reference, apologise, dispatch. Then find out why the notification did not arrive, and fix that.
Customer charged twice Confirm two distinct successful payments in Razorpay for the same cart. Refund one immediately, in full, before arguing about cause. Tell the customer it is done and when the money returns. Then investigate.
Settlement did not arrive Check the Razorpay settlement report and the settlement status — it may be on hold pending a KYC or bank detail mismatch. Client contacts Razorpay support with the settlement reference. Do not change bank details to "fix" it without confirming why it is held.
Refund fails Check the refund status in Razorpay and whether the merchant balance covers it. Top up if required and retry. If the card is closed, Razorpay will advise the bank route. Keep the customer informed in writing.
A key or password was exposed Rotate it immediately. Do not watch and wait. Regenerate in Razorpay, paste the new value into Shopify, verify one live transaction. Review the transaction log for the exposure window. Change the account password and check active sessions and connected apps.
Suspected account compromise Change the password, sign out all sessions, check staff accounts and connected apps for anything unrecognised. Rotate every key. Check that the settlement bank account has not been altered — this is the first thing an attacker changes.
Store is down Check Shopify's status page before anything else. If Shopify is up, check DNS and recent theme changes. Roll back the last theme change. Shopify keeps previous theme versions for exactly this.
Someone asks the client for an OTP Do not share it. End the call. Nobody legitimate asks for an OTP — not Razorpay, not Shopify, not you, not me. Tell the client this during training, in these words.
06

After launch — the weekly check

Ten minutes a week. Worth putting in the client's calendar during training.

  • Settlements reconcile — Razorpay settlement total for the week matches what landed in the bank.
  • Failed payments — a sudden rise usually means a broken checkout, not unlucky customers.
  • Orders stuck unpaid or unfulfilled for more than 48 hours.
  • Refunds issued — each one recognised and expected.
  • Stock counts against what is physically on the shelf.
  • Invoice series — still sequential, no gaps.
  • Staff and collaborator list — nobody with access who should not have it.
  • Shopify billing — the 2% third-party fee still not being charged on Magic Checkout orders.

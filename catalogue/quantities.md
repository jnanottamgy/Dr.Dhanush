# Net quantity for all 65 products

Read from the **catalogue name** for each product, per instruction: the quantity
in the name is the quantity he measures out.

Where a pack also prints a net quantity, the two are compared below. **They agree
everywhere they can be checked**, which is what makes the name trustworthy for
the products whose packs print nothing.

---

## The stripped fraction is ½ — proved, not assumed

Seven names arrived with a fraction lost in the filename (`kg-badam`,
`kgspecial-tea`, `coconut-oil-liter`, …). Two of those seven have a printed net
quantity on the pack, and both come out at a half:

| Name | Fraction implies | Pack declares | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kgspecial-tea` → ½ kg special tea | 500 g | **500gm** | ✔ |
| `coconut-oil-liter` → ½ liter coconut oil | 500 ml | **500 ml** | ✔ |

So `½` is the missing character, and it is applied to the rest of that group.
`100-shahi-jeera` is the odd one out — it lost a `g`, not a fraction, and sits
with the seventeen other 100 g whole spices.

## Where this leaves us

**55 of 65 products now have a net quantity.** A second pass over the labels
recovered four that the names had not carried — soapnut powder 200 g (MRP ₹110),
sikakai powder 200 g (MRP ₹120), pista 500 g, dry grapes 500 g.

Nine still have no quantity from any source, and every one of them is a bare tub
or bag:

- chia · sabja — clear tubs, no sticker at all
- flax · magaz · pumpkin · sunflower — name sticker only
- normal dates — bare poly bag
- special dates — pre-printed label, Net Weight and MRP boxes both left empty
- whole soapnut — bare tub

One more is printed but unreadable:

- **nellikai powder** — the label carries net contents and MRP, but the raw photo
  will not resolve them even at 20×. It is 200 g or 280 g. Asked for a close-up
  rather than guessed, per the standing rule.

Two packs declare a weight but left the MRP box blank: **pista** and **dry
grapes**. Those two prices ride along with the price list.

## What the measured quantity unlocks

If he weighs these out himself, **he is the packer** — and that supplies the rest
of the declarations from his own pack, which is already compliant:

| Declaration | Value |
|---|---|
| Net quantity | measured, per the table above |
| Packer name | Malnad Spices |
| Packer address | Devaramane, Horanadu Post, Kalasa Tq, Chikkamagaluru 577181 |
| FSSAI licence | 21223055000121 |
| Consumer care | 8431218956 · 9480956035 |
| Country of origin | India |
| Date of packing | the date he packs the order |
| MRP | from the price list — the one genuine gap |

So the roughly 25 products I had recorded as unlistable are **listable as soon as
the price list lands**, and for most of them the quantity was never actually
missing. That is a real change to the blocker list, not a softening of it.

Two things it does not fix:

1. **The physical pack still has to carry the label.** Listing data and a printed
   label are separate obligations. The tubs and poly bags currently go out with a
   name sticker only; as the packer he needs the full declaration on the pack he
   ships, not only on the website.
2. **Sealed third-party packs are not his to declare.** Kalpatharu, Sanjivni, QTF
   and Shree Durga carry their maker's declarations. The QTF sack carries none at
   all, so that one stays off the site until there is a retail pack.

## Soapnut and sikakai are not food

Soapnut powder, sikakai powder and the whole soapnuts are washing and hair-care
items. They need their own storefront section away from the spices and coffee,
and their HSN and GST rate will not match the food lines. Raised with Jnanottam —
the rate is his call as the CA. Oddly, the soapnut powder pouch carries an FSSAI
licence number (2127055000011), which is what a food would carry; that is the
maker's doing and worth a question, not a correction from us.

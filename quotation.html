The one thing Indian sellers get caught by

Shopify charges an extra 2% on Basic because you are in India. Shopify's own payment product, Shopify Payments, is not available in India. So every Indian store must plug in an Indian gateway like Razorpay — and Shopify then adds a 2% "third-party gateway" fee on every order that goes through its standard checkout. That is on top of what Razorpay charges. On ₹1,50,000 of monthly sales this is ₹3,000 + ₹540 GST = ₹3,540 a month.

Most agencies never mention this. We are telling you before you sign, and we have a way around it.

How we reduce it. Shopify's 2% applies only to orders that pass through Shopify's own checkout page. Orders completed through an approved quick-checkout — such as Razorpay Magic Checkout — currently fall outside it. Magic Checkout costs about 0.65% + GST, roughly a third of the 2%, and it also speeds up checkout and reduces drop-off at the payment step. On ₹1,50,000 of sales that is about ₹2,390 saved every month, ₹28,700 a year. We set this up as part of the build. To be straight with you: this is a gap in Shopify's fee rules, not a permanent guarantee, and Shopify could close it. We will tell you the day it changes.

How we will implement it

  1. Register the Shopify account in your name, with your email, your GSTIN and your card — so you own it outright and can claim the GST back as input credit.
  2. Buy and connect your domain, and set up a professional email address on it (for example orders@yourdomain.in).
  3. Configure India settings: INR, GST rates per product category, HSN codes, GST-compliant invoice format, delivery zones and charges that match how your own team operates, and free-delivery thresholds.
  4. Build the storefront, load your 30 products, and connect Razorpay and WhatsApp.
  5. Enable Razorpay Magic Checkout as the primary checkout to legally avoid the 2% fee, keeping Shopify's standard checkout as backup.
  6. Hand you the login. You hold the owner account; JTACS is added as staff. You can remove our access any day, and the store keeps running.

You asked for a WhatsApp Business account with a community people can join for updates and offers, and the ability to order through WhatsApp. That is genuinely achievable — but it needs two WhatsApp numbers, not one, and this is the detail most agencies get wrong and only discover after they have set it up.

The rule you must know before we start. WhatsApp has two separate products. The free WhatsApp Business App is the only one that can run Communities and groups. The paid WhatsApp Business Platform (API) is the only one that can send unlimited broadcasts and automatic order updates — but the moment a number is moved onto the API, that number can no longer use the Business App, and loses Communities and groups entirely.

A number cannot do both. So we set up two numbers, one for each job, both branded as your practice. Doing it this way from day one avoids a painful migration later — once a number goes to the API, moving it back means losing the number's history.

11

Compliance — what you must have in place

Selling Ayurvedic medicines online in India is legal and straightforward, but it is regulated. These are your responsibilities, not ours — we flag them now because ignoring them is what shuts stores down. Please confirm your position on each before we begin.

  • AYUSH licence. Ayurvedic, Siddha and Unani medicines fall under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and its 1945 Rules. A manufacturing licence is required if you make them; a retail licence if you only sell them. Razorpay will ask for this during KYC, and Shopify, Amazon and Meta all enforce it.
  • What you may and may not say. The Drugs and Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act, 1954 prohibits advertising that a product cures a listed disease — diabetes, cancer, heart conditions and others. General wellness language is permitted; disease-cure claims are not. This is the most common reason Ayurveda stores lose their payment gateway. We will write your product copy on the right side of this line, and flag anything in your existing material that reads as a disease-cure claim.
  • Labelling. Every product page must carry ingredients, net quantity, batch number, manufacturing and expiry dates, MRP inclusive of taxes, manufacturer name and address, and the licence number. We build these fields into the product template.
  • GST. Most Ayurvedic medicines attract 12% GST; some cosmetics and wellness products attract 18%. We configure HSN codes per product and set the store to issue GST-compliant invoices automatically. Please have your chartered accountant confirm the rate for each product before we load the catalogue.
  • Meta advertising policy. Health claims in WhatsApp templates and Meta ads are reviewed by Meta and rejected or banned if they overstate. We write templates to pass review the first time.
  • Store policies. Shipping, returns, refunds and privacy pages are legally required for an e-commerce business and for Razorpay approval. We draft all four; please have them reviewed by your legal advisor before go-live.

To be clear about our role. JTACS builds and operates your digital systems. We are not your legal or regulatory advisor, and we do not obtain licences on your behalf. Please have your chartered accountant and legal advisor confirm licensing, GST rates and product claims. What we guarantee is that we will not build anything that knowingly puts you on the wrong side of these rules, and that we will tell you when we see a risk.

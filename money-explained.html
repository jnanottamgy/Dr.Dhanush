01

The short version

If you read one page, read this one.

A customer pays on your website. The money goes to Razorpay, who handle payments for you. Razorpay takes their fee and sends the rest to your own bank account, usually about two working days later. It arrives on its own. You do not have to ask for it, claim it, or chase anybody.

Nobody else touches your money. It goes from the customer, to Razorpay, to your bank. JTACS never holds it, never sees it, and takes no commission on any sale. Our fee was the one-time charge for building the shop, and that is the end of it.

  1. Customer pays on malnadproducts.in — UPI, card, net banking or wallet
  2. Razorpay receives the money and the order appears in your Shopify account, marked Paid
  3. You pack and send it
  4. Razorpay pays into your bank, about two working days after the sale
02

How a customer pays you

They open your shop, choose their packs, and go to checkout. They type their name, address, phone number and email. Then they pay.

What they can pay with

  • UPI — Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, or scanning a QR code. This is how most Indian customers will pay you.
  • Cards — Visa, Mastercard, RuPay, Maestro
  • Net banking — straight from their bank account
  • Wallets — Paytm, Mobikwik, Freecharge and others

There is no Cash on Delivery, and that is deliberate. Every parcel you pack has already been paid for. You never send goods out and hope. You never have a parcel refused at the door and come back. You never chase anyone for money. This was agreed at the start and it is one of the quiet reasons this shop will be less trouble than it looks.

Delivery is added automatically at checkout, worked out from the weight of what they bought and how far it has to travel. You do not calculate it and you do not have to remember it.

04

What is taken out before it reaches you

Two companies charge you for running the shop. It is worth understanding them separately, because they are deducted in two completely different ways and this is the part people find confusing.

1. Razorpay — taken out of each payment

Razorpay charges a fee on every payment, plus 18% GST on that fee. It is deducted before the money reaches your bank, so what arrives is already net of it.

You can see the exact fee on every single payment in your Razorpay dashboard — what the customer paid, what Razorpay charged, what was sent to you. Nothing is hidden and nothing is estimated. If a figure ever looks wrong, it can be checked payment by payment.

2. Shopify — billed to you monthly

Shopify charges for the shop itself, and an extra fee on each sale because the payments are handled by Razorpay rather than by Shopify. This is not taken out of your settlements. It appears on your Shopify bill at the end of the month, like a phone bill.

So do not expect the two to add up in your bank statement. Razorpay's fee is already gone before the money arrives. Shopify's charge arrives separately as a bill. Both are normal, both are visible, and your accountant will want both.

05

When the money reaches your bank

Razorpay's standard is two working days after the payment. A sale on Monday morning is usually in your bank on Wednesday.

  • Working days only. Sundays, bank holidays, and the second and fourth Saturdays of the month do not count. A Friday sale will usually arrive on Tuesday.
  • Your own schedule is shown in your Razorpay dashboard. Two days is the standard, but the exact cycle is set on your account — check it there rather than assuming.
  • It arrives by itself. There is no button to press and no request to make.
  • Several payments arrive together. Razorpay groups a day's sales into one deposit, so three orders on Monday will usually land as one amount on Wednesday, not three separate credits. Match it against the dashboard, not against individual orders.

Keep your bank details correct with Razorpay. The money goes to whichever account is registered there. If that account ever changes — a new branch, a new account number — update Razorpay first, before the next sale.

06

What you actually have to do

Very little, and none of it is about collecting money.

  • Pack and send the order
    The only real work. Weigh it out, put the declarations on the pack, send it, and type the tracking number into Shopify.
  • Check your bank once a week
    Not to chase anything — just so you know the rhythm of what comes in and when.
  • Keep an eye on the Razorpay dashboard
    It shows every payment, every fee and every deposit. It is the honest record if a figure is ever questioned.
  • Nothing to collect, invoice or claim
    The money is already paid before you touch the parcel.
07

If you need to return someone's money

Open the order in Shopify and press Refund. Choose the whole order or a part of it. The money goes back to the customer the same way they paid, usually in five to seven working days.

The refund comes out of your next settlement — Razorpay deducts it from the following deposit rather than asking you for it. So a day with a refund will show a smaller deposit than the sales alone would suggest. That is normal and it is visible in the dashboard.

Refund the delivery charge only if the parcel never went out. Once it has been handed to the courier that money is genuinely spent, and your refund policy on the website says so.

08

Money you take at the shop counter

Sales made at the shop, at a market, or over the phone do not go through the website — that money comes to you directly, as it always has.

But enter them in Shopify anyway. On the phone app: Orders → Create order → add the items and the customer's name → mark it paid.

It takes a minute and it does three useful things: it raises a proper GST invoice, it keeps your month's totals real instead of showing only the website half of your business, and it puts the sale on the sheet your accountant gets.

09

GST, and the sheet for your accountant

Every price on the website already includes GST. You do not add tax at checkout and the customer is not charged anything on top of the price shown. Shopify works the tax out of the price and puts it on the invoice.

Once a month, export your orders from Shopify and send the file to Jnanottam. You get back a one-page sheet: your sales broken down by HSN code, with CGST, SGST and IGST worked out, in the shape your accountant needs for the GST return. Full instructions are in your owner's manual.

The last step, and it is yours

Everything is built and switched on, but no real money has gone through it yet. Until one payment has actually landed in your bank, nobody can honestly say the chain is proven — and we would rather prove it than assume it.

  1. Buy one small item from your own shop — a ₹50 or ₹80 pack — and pay with your own UPI.
  2. Check it appears in Shopify as a paid order, and in your Razorpay dashboard as a payment.
  3. Wait two working days and check your bank. The money should arrive, less Razorpay's fee.
  4. Tell Jnanottam what landed. That is the shop proven, from a customer's phone to your bank account.

It costs you almost nothing. You are paying yourself: the money goes out of your personal account and comes back into your business account two days later, minus a small fee. What you buy for that is certainty — and after that, every order is just this one, repeated.

One more thing is being improved in the background: a faster checkout from Razorpay that would reduce the fee on each sale. It is being set up by Razorpay's own team. It changes nothing about how you are paid — the money still goes to the same bank account on the same schedule — and the shop works perfectly well without it.

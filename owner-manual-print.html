01

What you have

Read this page first. Everything after it is the detail.

  • An online shop at malnadproducts.in with 57 products on it, in nine sections. A customer anywhere in India can open it on a phone, pick a pack size, pay, and give you their address.
  • Payment through Razorpay, straight into your own bank account. The customer pays on the shop itself — mobile number, a one-time code to confirm it, address, pay. The order then appears in Shopify by itself.

03

The day an order comes in

This is the routine. It takes about five minutes per parcel once you are used to it.

  1. Your phone buzzes. Open the Shopify app, go to Orders, open the new one.
  2. Check it says Paid. If it says Pending or Unpaid, the money has not arrived. Do not pack it. See the warning below.
  3. Open Print packing slip. It carries the customer’s name, address, phone and exactly what they bought.
  4. Weigh out each item to the quantity on the slip. If it says 500 g, it is 500 g.
  5. Write or stick the full declaration on every pack. Section 04 lists what has to be on it. This is the law, not a preference.
  6. Pack the parcel and hand it to India Post or your courier. Keep the tracking number.
  7. Back in the order, choose Fulfil, type the tracking number, and send. The customer gets an email with it automatically.
  8. Send the customer one WhatsApp with the tracking number. Takes ten seconds and it is the difference between a customer who waits patiently and one who phones you.

If an order ever says Cash on Delivery, do not pack it. This shop is prepaid only — that is how there are no refused parcels and no money to chase. Cash on delivery is switched off everywhere it can be switched off, so one should never reach you. If one somehow does: open the order, Cancel it, ring the customer on the number on the order to say the shop takes payment online only and they are welcome to order again, and tell Jnanottam it happened.

Why the WhatsApp matters. Shopify sends order and dispatch emails, but it does not send SMS unless you buy a separate app every month. We did not buy one, because you already have WhatsApp and every order carries a mobile number. One message from your phone does the job better than an automatic SMS would.

It matters more than it looks: some customers pay with a phone number and no email address, and for those the WhatsApp is the only message they get. Section 15 has that one.

04

What must be printed on every pack you fill

Because you weigh the goods out yourself, the law treats you as the packer. That makes these declarations yours to print, on the pack, not just on the website.

  • Name and full address of the packer — Malnad Spices, Devaramane, Horanadu Post, Kalasa Taluk, Chikkamagaluru 577181
  • Net quantity — the weight or volume inside, written as Net qty. 500 g
  • Retail sale price — the figure for that pack, written as MRP ₹140 (incl. of all taxes)
  • Month and year of packing
  • Best before — the date, or the number of months from packing
  • Consumer care — a name, a phone number and drjhrnd5@gmail.com
  • Country of origin — India
  • FSSAI licence number — 21223055000121

The penalty is counted per package, not per inspection. A hundred parcels going out with a name sticker only is a hundred offences, not one. This is the single biggest open risk in the whole shop, and it is the one thing no amount of website work can fix for you.

A rubber stamp or a printed sticker roll with the fixed lines on it — packer, address, FSSAI, country, care details — leaves you writing only the quantity, price and dates by hand. That is the cheapest way to close it.

Goods you only resell stay as they are. Sanjivni tea, the Kalpatharu and Aaradhya oils, the nine syrups, the Shree Durga oil and QTF tea arrive sealed by their own maker. Their declarations are the maker’s. Do not add yours over them and do not open them to re-pack.

05

Stock, and why there is nothing to update

You said it yourself: “stock, we’ll give according to orders.” The shop is built around that, so there is no stock figure anywhere for you to keep right.

On all 63 pack sizes, Shopify’s stock counting is switched off and each one is set to keep selling. So the shop never shows a number in stock, never says sold out, and will accept an order for anything on any day. The judgement about whether you can supply it stays with you, at the moment the order arrives — which is how you were working anyway.

If you cannot supply something for a while

Do not try to set its stock to zero. Set the product to Draft instead — section 08. It comes off the shop at once, and comes back the moment you switch it to Active. Nobody can order what is not there, and your old orders and your sales history are untouched.

Do not switch stock tracking on and leave it. It can be turned on for one product if you ever want a real count — but the moment it is on, the number has to be kept right, and when it reaches zero that product stops selling with no warning to you. A forgotten count at zero is how a shop quietly stops taking orders for its best seller. Only turn it on if you mean to maintain it, and tell Jnanottam if you do, because the monthly accountant’s sheet reads from the same place.

06

Changing a price

  1. Products → search the name → open it.
  2. Scroll to the pack sizes. Each size has its own price box. Change the one you mean.
  3. Save. The shop updates immediately.

If you change the price, change the MRP too. Further down the same page there is a Compliance section with an MRP box for each pack size. That is the figure the website prints as the legal declaration, and it is what has to match the number stamped on the pack the customer is holding. A selling price that no longer matches the printed MRP is a problem; leaving the two to drift apart is how it happens.

On the goods you only resell, the MRP box is deliberately empty. Leave it empty. Printing your own MRP on somebody else’s sealed pack is a false declaration.

All prices on the shop include GST. You do not add tax on top. Shopify works the tax out of the price for the invoice.

08

Taking something off sale

Open the product and change Status from Active to Draft. It disappears from the shop at once and comes back the moment you set it to Active again.

Do not delete a product. Old orders point at it. Deleting it damages your own sales history and the GST record for months already filed. Draft does everything you want and nothing you do not.

09

Selling at the shop counter or over the phone

Put it through Shopify as well, so your totals are your real totals.

  1. Orders → Create order (the + button on the phone app).
  2. Add the items and quantities, add the customer’s name and phone.
  3. Mark it as paid, with the method they used.

It raises a proper GST invoice, counts in your month’s figures, and appears in the accountant’s sheet alongside the website sales. Without this, the website shows half your business and the GST return shows the wrong number.

10

Refunds

  1. Open the order → Refund.
  2. Choose the items, or type a part amount.
  3. Send. The money goes back the way it came, usually in five to seven working days.

Refund the delivery charge too if the parcel never went out. If it did, you have already paid the courier and the refund policy on the website says so.

A refund is not the same as a cancellation. Cancel is for an order that has not been paid or has not gone out — it closes the order. Refund is for money that has already reached you.

12

Once a month, for the accountant

  1. Orders → Export → choose the month → CSV for Excel. It emails you a file.
  2. Send that file to Jnanottam.
  3. You get back a one-page sheet: sales broken down by HSN code, with CGST, SGST and IGST worked out. That is what the accountant needs for the GST return.

One thing on that sheet will not match Shopify. Shopify’s tax setting is currently working the tax out at twice the rate it should — 10% where it should be 5%. Customers are not overcharged, because prices include tax and the total they pay is the price on the shop. But the figure Shopify reports as tax is too high, and the difference comes out of your own margin at filing. On a ₹140 sale it declares ₹12.72 where ₹6.67 is right.

The sheet prints both numbers and the gap, so your accountant can see exactly what happened. It is a one-line fix in Settings, it is flagged, and it does not need to hold anything up.

13

Payments, and how the money reaches you

Razorpay approved the website on 8 October and the shop has been able to take real payments since. The money goes into your own bank account. Jnanottam never touches it.

What the customer sees

Since 9 October the shop uses Razorpay Magic Checkout. The customer stays on malnadproducts.in the whole way: they put in a mobile number, confirm it with a code, type the delivery address, and pay by UPI, card, net banking or wallet. The order then appears in Shopify by itself, with the address and the right delivery charge already on it.

When it reaches the bank

Razorpay holds each payment briefly and then deposits it, normally two working days after the sale. Sundays, bank holidays and the second and fourth Saturdays do not count. Several payments are usually batched into one deposit, so a ₹3,000 day may arrive as one line, not three. Two fees are taken — Razorpay’s out of each payment, and Shopify’s billed monthly like a phone bill — which is why the bank figure and the shop figure will not be identical. How the money works sets that out properly with a worked example.

Checking a payment

Every sale shows up in two places: the order in Shopify, and the payment in your Razorpay dashboard. They should always agree, and the exact fee charged on each payment is shown against it in Razorpay. If one side ever shows a sale the other does not, say so straight away rather than waiting for the month end.

One thing is still open, and it is honest to say out loud: no real money has gone through yet.

The chain — customer pays, Razorpay deposits, it appears in your bank — has been tested at every step except the last. The way to close it is to buy one small pack from your own shop, with your own card or UPI, and watch it land. You are paying yourself, so it costs you only the fee, and it is the only thing that proves the money arrives.

14

Things not to touch

Each of these quietly breaks something that took a long time to get right.

01

Magic Shipping, in the Razorpay dashboard

Its own warning says it bypasses all shipping settings from your platform. Turning it on throws away the four zones and twenty-eight weight-based rates and starts charging the wrong postage on every parcel. Leave it off.

02

Publishing a theme

There are four designs in the library and only one is live — Magic Checkout - Malnad Spices — build, marked Current theme. The others look nearly identical in the editor. Publishing one replaces your shop’s design and can take the checkout with it. Never press Publish.

03

Uninstalling the Razorpay app

The app named for your merchant ID is what runs the checkout, and it was built for this shop specifically. Removing it takes the checkout away, and getting it back took three weeks.

04

Republish Cart, in the Razorpay dashboard

It pushes a different shopping cart onto your website, in Razorpay’s blue rather than your green. Nobody asked for it and it is not needed.

05

Deleting products, collections or pages

Set products to Draft instead. Deleting a collection breaks the links in the Shop menu; deleting a page breaks the links in the footer.

06

Switching on Cash on Delivery

It is off on purpose, everywhere it can be set — in Shopify and on Razorpay’s side both. The shop is prepaid only, which is why there are no refused parcels and no money chasing. Switching it back on undoes that in one click.

07

Test mode, under Payments

The shop keeps looking completely normal and takes no money at all. If sales suddenly stop, this is the first thing to check.

08

Switching on stock tracking

Section 05. A count left at zero makes a product unbuyable and tells you nothing about it.

If the checkout ever breaks completely — customers reach the last step and cannot pay — there is one step back. The design called Malnad Spices — build is kept in the library for exactly this. Publishing it returns the shop to Shopify’s own checkout, which is slower and costs a little more per sale but works. Ring Jnanottam first if you can; this is the emergency answer if you cannot reach anyone.

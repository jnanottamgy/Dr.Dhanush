Subject

Own Storefront & Owner Dashboard

A custom-built online store and an owner's dashboard, with WhatsApp community and ordering. Built on your own infrastructure — no platform rent and no commission on your sales.

01

What we are building

Three things, on infrastructure you own outright. Read this page if you read nothing else.

  • A storefront. Your own website where customers browse your coffee and spices, choose a pack size, and pay online. Fast on a phone, built to be found on Google, and built around where the produce comes from — provenance is what sells Malnad goods.
  • An owner dashboard. One screen that answers "how did we do today" — sales, orders waiting to be packed, what is selling, what is running out of stock, and a clean export for your accountant.
  • A WhatsApp channel. A community customers join for offers, plus the ability to order inside WhatsApp itself. Those orders arrive in the same dashboard automatically — nobody re-types them.

Why building your own is the right call here. A rented platform would charge roughly ₹21,000 a year in subscription plus a 2% cut of every order for using an Indian payment gateway. On ₹1,50,000 of monthly sales that 2% alone is ₹42,480 a year, rising every year you grow. Your own build has neither. You pay for hosting — a few thousand rupees a month — and nothing per sale. The trade is that the system is yours to look after, which is what Sections 06 to 08 are about.

One assumption to confirm before we build. This plan is engineered for a launch of 20–40 orders a day, designed with headroom to 1,500 orders a day so a festival rush or a post that travels does not take the store down. If you expect materially more than that from day one, tell us now — it changes the database and hosting tier, and it is far cheaper to decide before we build than after.

02

The storefront — what customers get

A  ·  Catalogue built for produce

  • Products with real variants. One coffee, sold as 250 g / 500 g / 1 kg, and as beans / coarse / fine grind — each with its own price, stock count and barcode. Same for spices by weight. Customers pick on one page instead of hunting through six listings.
  • Collections — Coffee, Spices, Honey & Estate Goods, Gift Boxes, and seasonal sets for harvest and festivals.
  • Provenance content. Estate story, altitude, harvest month, roast date, single-origin notes. For Malnad produce this is not decoration — it is the reason someone pays your price instead of a supermarket's.
  • Search and filters by category, weight, roast and price, with related-product suggestions on every page.
  • Gift and bulk options — gift notes at checkout and a simple bulk-enquiry form for shops and offices.

B  ·  Checkout

  • Prepaid, through Razorpay — UPI, cards, net banking and wallets. UPI carries no charge at all; cards cost 2% plus GST. Nothing ships before the money has cleared.
  • Guest checkout by default, with an optional account for order history and faster repeat purchases. Forcing account creation is the single biggest cause of abandoned carts.
  • Delivery charges by pin code, free above a cart value you choose, with your own delivery arrangement behind it.
  • GST-compliant invoice generated automatically, with HSN codes and a sequential invoice number per financial year.

C  ·  Built to be found and to load fast

  • Mobile first. Most of your traffic will be a phone on a patchy connection. Pages are built to open in about a second on 4G.
  • SEO from the start — clean URLs, product structured data, sitemap, and a Google Business Profile, so "malnad coffee online" can find you.
  • Analytics so you can see where visitors came from and what they bought.

Deliberately two layers. One is a full operations console; the other is the single screen you actually open with your morning coffee.

Layer 1

Operations console

The working tool for whoever runs the shop day to day.

  • Orders — search, filter, open, mark packed and dispatched
  • Products — add, edit, price, publish or hide
  • Stock — counts per variant, low-stock warnings
  • Refunds and cancellations, processed properly through Razorpay
  • Customers and their order history
  • Discount codes and offers
Layer 2

Owner View Bespoke

One screen, built for you, answering the questions you actually ask.

Both read the same database, so they can never disagree. The operations console is a proven open-source admin we configure and secure rather than write from scratch — that is deliberate, and Section 05 explains why. The Owner View is built specifically for you.

04

WhatsApp — community and ordering

You asked whether WhatsApp orders have to be typed into the dashboard by hand. They do not. Here is how they arrive on their own.

The rule that shapes this, and it is not negotiable. WhatsApp has two separate products. The free WhatsApp Business App is the only one that can run a Community. The paid WhatsApp Business Platform (API) is the only one that can broadcast without limit and send automatic order updates — and the moment a number moves to the API it can no longer use the Business App, and loses Communities entirely.

One number cannot do both. So we set up two numbers from day one, both branded as your business. Doing this later means losing a number's history, which is why it is decided now.

Three ways an order can arrive from WhatsApp — all land in the dashboard automatically
RouteHow it works for the customerHow the order reaches youPhase
Link out Day one They chat, and get a link to a ready-filled cart on the website. They pay there. It is an ordinary website order. No extra machinery at all. 1
Order inside the chat A form opens inside WhatsApp — pick products, quantity, address, confirm. No browser, no app switch. WhatsApp posts the completed form to our server, which creates the order and marks its source as WhatsApp. 3
Pay inside the chat Razorpay is linked to your WhatsApp business account, so UPI or card completes without leaving the conversation. Razorpay confirms the payment to our server and the order is marked paid. 3
Manual entry For phone orders and odd cases, staff create an order from the dashboard. Always available — but the exception, not the workflow. 1

The in-chat form is a WhatsApp feature called Flows. There is one engineering constraint worth naming because it dictates how we build it: WhatsApp requires our server to acknowledge a submitted form within three seconds or the customer sees an error. So we accept the form instantly and do the real work — stock check, order creation, confirmation message — a moment later in the background. Section 06 covers that queue.

What we set up

  1. Number 1 — Community, on the free app: business profile, catalogue, an announcement group and topic groups for Harvest Updates, Offers, and Recipes & Brewing.
  2. Number 2 — ordering and updates, on the API: Meta business verification, green tick application, and the shared inbox your staff work from.
  3. Message templates submitted to Meta for approval: order confirmed, payment received, dispatched with tracking, delivered, abandoned-cart reminder, re-order reminder, and festival offers.
  4. Join links and QR codes for the website, order confirmations, and a printed insert in every parcel — that insert is how a one-time buyer becomes a repeat one.
  5. Consent capture at every entry point, recorded and timestamped. Meta bans numbers that message people who did not opt in, and a banned number is very hard to recover.
05

How it is built

The single most important engineering decision in this project is what we do not write ourselves.

We do not hand-write the shop engine. Carts, order states, stock locking, partial refunds — that is where custom stores fail, and none of it is unique to you. We build on Medusa, an open-source commerce core used in production worldwide, free and MIT-licensed, with no fees of any kind. It brings the order and stock logic already proven by thousands of stores, plus the operations console in Section 03. We then build what is yours: the storefront, the Owner View, and the WhatsApp layer. Fewer of our own bugs, and security fixes to the core come from its community rather than from us noticing.

The six things that make those numbers true

  1. Browsing is served from cache, not from the database. Product pages are pre-built and pushed to servers near the customer. A post that goes viral hits the cache, and the cache does not run out of breath. This is the single biggest reason the numbers above are achievable on modest hardware.
  2. Connection pooling on the database. The classic way a small store dies under load is running out of database connections while the machine sits idle. A pooler sits in front so hundreds of simultaneous requests share a small, safe number of connections.
  3. Slow outside services never block a customer. WhatsApp messages, confirmation emails and invoice generation go onto a background queue. If Meta is slow this evening, your customer still checks out in three seconds; the message goes a minute later.
  4. The application scales sideways automatically. Traffic climbs, more copies of the app start; traffic falls, they stop. You pay for what you use, and a festival morning does not need anyone awake to handle it.
  5. Stock is decremented inside a locked transaction. If two people buy the last 500 g pack in the same second, exactly one succeeds and the other is told it just sold out. This is a correctness guarantee, not a best effort.
  6. Rate limiting and bot protection on checkout and login, so a scripted attack cannot exhaust stock or hammer the payment gateway.

We prove this before launch, not after. In the final week we run a scripted load test simulating 2,000 concurrent shoppers and 100 checkouts a minute for a sustained period, and we do not go live until it passes with the targets above intact. We also restore the database from backup into a scratch environment and check it comes back clean — an untested backup is not a backup. Both results are written up and handed to you.

07

Making sure the money is always right

The worst failure in an online shop is not a slow page. It is a customer who paid and whose order vanished. This is how that is prevented.

The safeguards, plainly

  • Card details never touch your system. Payment happens on Razorpay's own page. We receive a yes or a no, never a card number — which removes an entire category of risk from your business.
  • Every message from Razorpay is signature-checked. A forged "payment successful" message cannot create a paid order, because it will not carry Razorpay's signature.
  • A repeated confirmation cannot create a second order. Payment services deliberately retry messages. Each one carries an identifier we record, so the same payment processed five times still produces exactly one order.
  • Stock cannot go negative. Reserved at checkout, committed on payment, released automatically if payment never completes.
  • Every change is written to an audit trail — who changed what, when. If a figure is ever questioned, it can be answered from the record rather than from memory.
  • Refunds go through Razorpay properly and are recorded against the order, so your books and the gateway always agree.
08

Security, backups and keeping it running

Security

What protects the site

  • Encrypted connection everywhere, enforced
  • No card data stored, ever
  • Passwords and API keys held in a managed secret store, never in the code
  • Dashboard access by named account with two-factor authentication and roles — staff cannot see what owners see
  • Rate limiting and bot protection on checkout and sign-in
  • Dependencies scanned for known vulnerabilities on every change
Continuity

If something goes wrong

  • Automated daily database backups with point-in-time recovery
  • A restore rehearsed and verified before launch, not assumed
  • Uptime monitoring that alerts within a minute of the site going down
  • Error tracking, so faults are found before customers report them
  • Every deployment reversible — a bad release is rolled back, not debugged live
  • A written runbook so recovery does not depend on one person's memory

Being straight with you about ownership. On a rented platform, an outage is the platform's problem. On your own build it is yours, and the saving in Section 01 is the other side of exactly that coin. The measures above are how we keep that risk small: managed services wherever someone else can carry the burden, monitoring so problems surface early, and tested backups so the worst case is hours rather than a business. What none of it removes is the need for someone to be responsible for the system after launch — which is a commercial matter, and is dealt with in Section 12.

Compliance, built into the product

You hold the licences. Our job is to make sure the software cannot quietly put you in breach of them.

The mechanism, not a promise. Every declaration the law requires on a food listing is a mandatory field on the product record. A product with any of them missing cannot be published to the storefront — the system refuses. That way compliance does not depend on anyone remembering.

How we will run the build

Six weeks in five phases, each ending in something you can see and sign off. Nothing moves forward until the previous gate is cleared.

What actually sets the date. The code is not the slow part. Razorpay verification, Meta business verification, and your product photographs and descriptions are — these run on other people's clocks, not ours. That is why Phase 0 starts everything in parallel on day one. Realistically, the timeline below holds if your catalogue material reaches us in week one; every week it is late is a week added at the end.

What we need from you

  • Product list with every declaration — name, description, net quantity, MRP, manufacturer or packer details, packing date, best before, country of origin, HSN code. This is the item most likely to delay launch, so it is worth starting today.

What you own at the end

Yours outright

Accounts and data

Every account — domain, hosting, database, Razorpay, Meta, storage — is opened in your name, on your card, from day one. JTACS is added as a user and can be removed any day without the shop stopping. Your customer and order data is yours, exportable in a standard format whenever you ask.

Handed over

Code and documentation

The full source code in a repository under your account, with a runbook covering deployment, key rotation, backup restoration, reading the logs and what to do when the site is down. Plus a recorded ninety-minute training session for you and your staff.

Why the runbook matters more than it sounds. A custom system is only as safe as the knowledge of how to run it. Written down and handed over, that knowledge survives holidays, illness and changes of supplier — including us. We would rather you were never stuck than that you were dependent.

01

What this is

You already film and upload long videos to YouTube. Most of that footage is never seen a second time. This takes each video, finds the moments that stand up on their own, and rebuilds them as finished vertical Reels — four a week, every week.

Nothing new gets filmed. No shoot days, no scripting, no presenters. The footage exists and is already paid for. This is about getting a second and third audience out of it, on the platform where short video actually travels.

The hard part is the choosing, not the cutting. Anybody can crop a video to vertical. What decides whether a Reel is watched is finding the forty seconds inside a thirty-minute video that works with none of the context around it, and opening on the line that stops a thumb. Software picks the loudest moment. That is not the same as the best one.

02

What “fully edited” means — every clip, every time

Fifteen things are done to each clip. This is the list, so there is no doubt later about what is included.

A  ·  Finding and framing

  • The whole video watched, and the moments chosen by judgement. You get a timestamped shortlist before any editing starts.
  • Reframed vertical at 9:16 with the subject tracked and kept centred through the shot — not a fixed crop that leaves a head half out of frame when somebody moves.
  • Built inside Instagram’s safe area. The app covers the bottom of the screen with its own buttons and the right-hand edge with the action bar. Text placed without allowing for that gets hidden, which is the commonest fault in reposted clips.

B  ·  The cut

  • A hook built into the first two seconds — the opening line chosen deliberately and on-screen hook text over it. Nearly all the drop-off happens here.
  • Tightened throughout: dead air, false starts, repeated words, long pauses and throat-clearing taken out, with cuts placed so they are not jarring.
  • Paced for the platform — a visual change every few seconds, because a static talking head loses people however good the words are.
  • Pack photographs and product shots cut in where the speech refers to something. Your seventy pack photographs are already on hand for this, so a clip about cardamom shows the cardamom.

C  ·  Captions, picture and sound

  • Captions burned in and timed to the speech, in a consistent brand font and colour, and read through and corrected by hand. Automatic captions get Kannada words and product names wrong every time, and wrong captions on a food product read as carelessness.
  • Colour corrected — exposure and white balance evened out, so clips from different videos and different light still look like one channel.
  • Audio levelled to Instagram’s loudness standard, with background noise reduced, harsh consonants softened, and music ducked underneath the voice rather than fighting it.
  • Music from Instagram’s own licensed library, so there is no copyright claim and no clip silently muted a week after posting.

D  ·  Finishing and delivery

  • On-screen text and graphics where they earn their place — the product name, a price, a lower third naming who is speaking.
  • An end card with the shop address, so a clip that travels actually sends people somewhere.
  • A cover frame chosen for the grid, so the profile does not fill up with frozen mid-blink faces.
  • A written caption with hashtags for each post, and the shop link where it fits.
  • Exported to platform specification — 1080 × 1920, correct frame rate and bitrate, so Instagram does not re-compress it into mush — then posted and scheduled for you, or handed over as files. Your choice, same price.

One round of changes on every clip is included — a different hook, a tighter cut, a caption fix, a different cover. What is not included is a second and third round on the same clip, or a complete re-edit built on a different moment of the video. That is charged as an extra clip, at the rate below.

Why four a week and not one

Instagram rewards a steady rhythm far more than it rewards any single good Reel. One clip a week is not enough for the platform to start showing you to people who do not already follow you. Four a week is comfortably past that line, and it means that when one clip does travel, there are fifteen more behind it for the people it brings.

Start with the back catalogue. Every video already on the channel is footage you have paid for and never reused. Ten clips out of it gives Instagram a fortnight of posts on day one, and shows you what your audience responds to before the monthly work begins.

04

How each month runs

  1. You upload as normal. Nothing changes about how you film or post on YouTube. Send the link, or give me view access to the channel — whichever suits you.
  2. I watch the full video and pick the moments, and send you a timestamped shortlist of what I intend to cut. One line each, so it takes a minute to read.
  3. You say yes, or swap one out. That is the only thing I need from you in the whole month, and it takes two minutes.
  4. The clips are delivered through the month, not dumped at the end, so posting stays regular. Four working days from your approval for the first batch.
  5. One round of changes on each clip, as set out in section 02.
  6. A short note at the end of the month — which clips were watched to the end, which were saved and shared, and what that suggests for the next month. Instagram’s own figures, not my opinion.
05

What this does not include, and what it cannot promise

Not included

Filming or scripting anything new · running the Instagram account — replying to comments and direct messages is a separate job at a separate price · paid promotion and its ad spend · YouTube thumbnails or long-form editing · posting to any platform other than Instagram, which is quoted separately · designing a logo or brand identity · licensing commercial music from outside Instagram’s own library · voice-overs, presenters or actors · subtitles in a second language · animation or 3D work.

What cannot be promised

No honest person can promise you views, followers or a Reel going viral. Instagram decides what it shows, and changes how it decides, often. What can be promised is that sixteen clips go out every month, on time, chosen with judgement and finished to the standard set out in section 02 — which is the whole of what anybody actually controls.

Short video is a rhythm, not a campaign. The first month usually looks quiet. What tends to happen is that somewhere around the second or third month one clip travels much further than the rest, and that is the one that brings people to the shop. If you are expecting a result in three weeks, this is the wrong thing to buy — and it is better to say that now than to take your money and explain it afterwards.

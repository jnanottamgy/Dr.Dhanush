Short vertical clips cut from your existing YouTube videos, edited for Instagram

01

What this is

You already film and upload long videos to YouTube. Most of that footage is never seen twice. This takes each video and pulls the two or three moments out of it that work on their own, cuts them vertical, captions them, and puts them on Instagram as Reels.

Nothing new gets filmed. No new scripting, no new shoot days. The footage already exists and is already paid for — this is about getting a second and third audience out of it, on the platform where short video actually travels.

The work is the choosing, not the cutting. Anybody can crop a video to vertical. The part that decides whether a Reel is watched is finding the forty seconds in a thirty-minute video that stands up without the context around it, and opening on the line that stops a thumb. That judgement is what this is.

02

What every clip gets

  • The moment chosen by watching the whole video, not by letting software pick the loudest thirty seconds.
  • Cut vertical at 9:16 and framed so the speaker stays centred, rather than cropped with heads cut off.
  • A hook in the first two seconds — the opening line and the on-screen text that makes someone stop. This is where a Reel is won or lost.
  • Burned-in captions, read through and corrected by hand. Automatic captions get Kannada words and product names wrong, and wrong captions on a food product look careless.
  • Music and sound levelled, from Instagram’s own licensed library so there is no copyright claim and no muted audio.
  • A cover frame chosen for the grid, so your profile does not fill up with frozen mid-blink faces.
  • A caption written for the post, with hashtags, and the shop link where it fits.
  • Delivered ready to post — or posted and scheduled for you, if you would rather not touch it. Your choice, same price.

Why three a week rather than one

Instagram rewards a steady rhythm far more than it rewards any single good Reel. One clip a week is not enough signal for the platform to start showing you to people who do not already follow you; three a week is the point where that usually begins. The Starter pack exists because some people want to test before committing, and that is fair — but it is a test, not a strategy.

Start with the back catalogue. Every video already on the channel is unmined footage that cost nothing extra. Ten clips out of it gives Instagram a month of posts on day one, and tells you what your audience responds to before you commit to a monthly pack.

04

How each month runs

  1. You upload as normal. Nothing changes about how you film or post on YouTube. Send the link, or give me view access to the channel — whichever you prefer.
  2. I watch the full video and pick the moments. You get a short list of what I intend to cut, with timestamps, before any editing starts. One line each, so it takes a minute to read.
  3. You say yes, or swap one out. Takes you two minutes and it is the only thing I need from you in the whole month.
  4. The clips are edited and delivered through the month rather than dumped at the end, so posting stays regular. Four working days from your approval for the first batch.
  5. One round of changes on each clip is included — a different hook, a tighter cut, a caption fix. Beyond that, or a complete re-edit on a different moment, is charged as an extra clip.
  6. A short note at the end of the month — which clips were watched, which were saved and shared, and what that suggests for next month. Instagram’s own figures, not my opinion.
05

What this does not include, and what it cannot promise

Not included

Filming or scripting anything new · running the Instagram account — replying to comments and direct messages is a separate job and a separate price · paid promotion and its ad spend · YouTube thumbnails or long-form editing · designing a logo or brand identity · posting to platforms other than Instagram, which is quoted separately · licensing commercial music outside Instagram’s own library · voice-overs, presenters or actors.

What cannot be promised

No honest person can promise you views, followers or a Reel going viral. Instagram decides what it shows and changes how it decides, often. What can be promised is that the clips go out on time, every week, chosen with judgement and finished properly — and that is the only part of it anybody controls.

Short video is a rhythm, not a campaign. The first month usually looks quiet. What tends to happen is that around the second or third month one clip travels much further than the rest, and that one is what brings people to the shop. If you are expecting a result in three weeks, this is the wrong thing to buy — and it is better to say so now than to take your money and explain it later.
